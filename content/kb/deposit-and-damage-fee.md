A refundable damage fee (deposit) is required per room, per stay, at move-in.

## Deposit amount

Settled by Bea on 08/12/26. It depends on whether the stay is transient or on a
signed lease, which is why the website's "varies by location" reads as vague — it
varies by **stay type**, not by property:

| Stay type | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Transient | **$100** | per room, **all properties** |
| Signed lease | **$250** | per unit |

**The website is wrong twice over.** The homepage says the fee "varies depending
on the location and length of stay" and the FAQ says "the amount varies by
location, so contact your preferred property" — both point a guest at the
property for a figure that is actually uniform. The Deposit Terms & Conditions
page's flat **$100** was correct for transient stays all along.

You can now quote these directly. Say which one applies: a lease applicant told
"$100" will dispute the other $150 at signing.

## What the deposit is for

The deposit is held to ensure compliance with the Stayable rules and
regulations. It is **forfeited in full** if the guest:

- smokes in the room
- damages the property
- fails to vacate by 11:00 AM
- carries an unpaid balance
- leaves trash or pet waste
- violates other house rules

Stained linens, pets in a non-pet room, room damage, and illegal activity found
at the check-out inspection are all causes for forfeiture.

## Departure cleaning fees (non-refundable)

These are separate from the deposit and are not refundable:

| Length of stay | Departure cleaning fee |
|---|---|
| 14 or more days | $50 |
| 28 or more days | $100 |

## Notice required to get the deposit back

The guest must give notice of non-renewal **no less than 40 hours before the
fully paid check-out date** on the current reservation — in practice, **by
5:00 PM two days before scheduled check-out**.

Missing that deadline is the most common reason a guest who did nothing else
wrong does not get their deposit back. Flag it at the time of booking for
weekly and monthly stays.

## How refunds are issued

Refunds go back to the original card on file. If that fails, a digital prepaid
card is issued by The Bancorp Bank (FDIC-insured, licensed by Mastercard
International), which **expires twelve months after issuance**.
