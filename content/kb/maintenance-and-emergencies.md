## Life-threatening emergency

**Call 911 immediately.** Nothing below replaces that.

## After-hours escalation — who to call

There is a formal call tree. **The live per-person numbers are NOT in this
knowledgebase, on purpose** — see the note at the end of this section for why.

**Open the Smartsheet named `Emergency Call Tree SOP 2026`.** That is the single
source of truth for who is on call and on what number.

The shape of it, so you know what you are looking at before you open it:

1. **Property staff first** — each property has between one and four named
   contacts, in order. Work down the list.
2. **Then Bianca**, who appears on every property's list.
3. **Then Shay**, last on every property's list.

Bianca and Shay are the company-wide backstop, not property contacts — if you
have reached them, the property tier did not answer.

### The two routing decisions

- **If the situation sounds urgent → call the property.** Work the list.
- **If it is not urgent → leave a note for the morning shift.** Do not wake the
  on-call tier for something that can wait.

Judging which is which is the job. The categories below are the ones the SOP
treats as urgent.

### What counts as an after-hours emergency

**Safety / police / medical**

- Guest injury or medical emergency
- Fight, violence, or threat
- Fire or smoke
- Police on site
- Suspicious person or break-in — **advise the guest to call the police**
- Domestic disturbance — **advise the guest to call the police**

**Flood, water leak, or major maintenance**

- Active water leak or flooding
- Ceiling collapse
- No electricity in multiple rooms
- No water
- Sewage backup
- AC outage affecting multiple units

**Guest locked out / access issue — critical only**

- Guest cannot enter the property at night
- Door lock not working
- Smart lock failure

**Fire alarm / emergency alarm**

- Alarm going off
- Fire department on site

**Weather / disaster**

- Power outage
- Flood warning
- Evacuation

Two of these — suspicious person/break-in and domestic disturbance — carry an
explicit instruction to **advise the guest to call the police** rather than
handling it through the call tree. That is not a suggestion in the SOP.

### Lakeland runs a shared on-call line, and it is the better pattern

Lakeland routes through a single **on-call emergency phone: 863-210-6420**, with
a named person rostered by day (Mon–Tue, Wed, Thu–Fri, Sat–Sun).

This is worth knowing for two reasons. Practically: for Lakeland you dial one
number regardless of the day, and the roster decides who picks up. Structurally:
it is the only property whose after-hours contact **does not change when staff
change**, so it is the only one that cannot silently go stale.

### Why the numbers are not copied into this page

Every other property's tree is a list of individual mobile numbers. Copying those
here would create a second, un-maintained copy that starts drifting the moment
somebody leaves or swaps a shift — and a wrong number in an *emergency*
procedure is worse than no number, because it costs time at the exact moment
there is none. The Smartsheet is maintained; this page is not the place to
duplicate it.

## The number to give a GUEST

The above is staff escalation. A guest reporting an after-hours problem should be
given the property's **AKIA number** — see
[Property directory](/kb/property-directory).

**The after-hours number is not published on the website — it is posted on the
lobby door.** The Rules & Regulations refer to "the after-hours number posted on
the lobby door" and call it **the only approved after-hours contact method**. So
the digits are not online and a guest cannot look them up remotely, but they are
physically on site. Give them the AKIA number directly rather than sending
somebody to read a door at midnight.

**After-hours starts at 11:00 PM ET** in the Rules, and the conditions they name
for using it are: a major water leak, loss of heat or AC, an electrical hazard, a
non-functioning lock, or any immediate risk to safety or property. That list is
narrower than the staff call-tree categories below, which is the point — it is
what a *guest* is told qualifies.

## Routine maintenance

A dripping faucet, a burned-out bulb, or a minor appliance issue goes through
the online maintenance system during business hours, not the emergency line.

## Conditions the guest must report

Guests are required to take action to promptly eliminate any dangerous
condition on the premises. A hazard noticed and not reported is the guest's
responsibility as well as ours.

## What the company does not take responsibility for

The Rules & Regulations state these in capitals, which is worth knowing before
a guest raises one:

- The owner is not liable or responsible for storage or disposition of a
  guest's personal property.
- The company is not responsible for the safety of any valuable left in the
  guest room.
- There is no guaranteed in-room phone, security, or overnight staff. Guests
  waive liability claims arising from these limited services.

## Do not contact staff at home

A guest may not knock on, approach, or otherwise attempt to contact a manager
or staff member at their personal residence — **$100 per occurrence**, and it
may carry a 7-day notice to vacate. If a guest cannot reach anyone, the answer
is the after-hours number, not a home visit.
